**3. STRONGEST IDEA IN THE KILL PILE — and one kill that is wrong in its conclusion, not its reasoning**

Two kills each recorded a *positive* result about DRIVE as collateral and then discarded it with the candidate:

- From the developing-single-print kill: "an unconditional, zero-information time-conditioned uncap (+0.0891 → +0.1031R, DD 12.1 → 10.8R, MAR 1.82 → 2.36, **2023 turned positive**)." That is a +16% expectancy, −11% drawdown, +30% MAR improvement that fixes DRIVE's only stated weakness, and it was measured as a *control arm*.
- From c-period-close-outside-ib: "drive earns **+0.523R** on those 298 NQ days versus **−0.124R** on the other 608." A 0.65R spread from one closed 11:00 bar, no lookahead.

The c-period kill is right that a same-direction *re-entry* an hour later at a worse price is worthless, and right that most of the spread is mark-to-market (the same objection that killed Form 3 and the C-close confirmation: "the kept cohort is simply the trades already +0.274R in the money"). But it killed the *entry construction* and threw away the *sorting statistic*. The small change that fixes it: use the 11:00 C-period state not to enter but to decide **target and hold** on the trade DRIVE already holds — and pre-register it against the correct dumb benchmark, "trade is green at 11:00", plus the zero-information uncap above, since those two are what actually have to be beaten.

**4. MOST PROMISING REMAINING DIRECTION**

**Exit, target and hold architecture on DRIVE — not entry filtering.** Twenty candidates failed with the *same* signature: %trades removed ≈ %losses removed, rejected cohort profitable. That is not twenty independent failures, it is one finding — **DRIVE's losers are not identifiable at 10:00 from any session statistic**, profile, volatility, volume, width or location. Meanwhile the only large separations anywhere in the file are time-conditioned and post-entry: 72.5% of losing R realized before o+120, the uncap result above, and (from the search notes, never mechanised) the max(band, VWAP) trailing stop at drawdown 21%→12% with skew −1.24→+1.29.

Why nobody tested it: the project's own machinery cannot express it. The S8 kill says it outright — "tools/filters.py's separation test is a boolean mask over a fixed `r` vector and cannot express it." Every pre-registration template demands "(%losses removed) − (%trades removed) > 8 points", which is undefined for a rule that *mutates* trade outcomes instead of deleting trades. So twenty proposals were shaped to fit the tool, and DRIVE's 2R target and unconditional hold-to-close — never swept once — sat untouched while the entry gate got twenty attempts.

Four of the search notes' own top-ranked picks are in this same untested class and never reached the candidate pipeline at all: **information-discreteness/FIP** (sign-count of the 30 M1 bars inside DRIVE's own window — recommended first by two independent angles), **one-timeframing as a hold/exit condition** (angle 1's #1; the only zero-parameter mechanical definition Dalton actually supplies), the **VWAP trailing stop**, and the **two-window sign-agreement** filter. Add a fifth from angle 12 that costs one afternoon: **skrecok's Hartigan dip test** (p=0.99 on 2,669 DAX sessions) as a standing pre-screen — it falsifies any proposed discrete session taxonomy before a backtest is written.
