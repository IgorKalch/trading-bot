  zero costs                   229   50.2%   0.98   -0.007

This is a different KIND of failure. ORB has no edge to lose: at zero cost it
is a coin flip, PF 0.98. The retest model at zero cost is PF 1.38 with a 57.9%
win rate at 1R — there is a real edge in the raw signal, and transaction costs
consume all of it, because 3 points of spread and slippage are 11% of a
27-point R.

That is exactly the problem Hougaard spends half his video on: he picks a
broker at 0.4 spread on the FTSE precisely because scalping on a 2-point
spread means starting every trade far behind.

--- D. WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH ---
38 trades over 75 sessions. The standard error on expectancy here is around
0.16R, which is the size of the whole zero-cost effect. This is a lead worth
chasing, not a result. It needs, in order:
  1. the real session spread on GER40, measured with the market open;
  2. more M1 history than this broker will give (another data source);
  3. an out-of-sample split, impossible on 3.5 months.
A floor on 1R (min_stop_points 25) lifts real-cost expectancy to +0.101R on
16 trades, which is far too few to mean anything and is listed only so the
next person does not rediscover it and get excited.
