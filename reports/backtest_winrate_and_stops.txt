===== BUYING AN 80% WIN RATE, AND WHAT IT COSTS =====

Leg 1 (retest, NQ, 349 trades). Baseline: WR 57.6%, PF 1.31, +0.133R, DD 10.7R.

--- THE ARITHMETIC FIRST ---
At a fixed target, win rate is not earned, it is BOUGHT - with a nearer target
or a wider stop. The break-even win rate for a target of k times risk is
1/(1+k) before costs:

  target 1.00R -> break even at 50.0%    we have 57.6%   margin +7.6pp
  target 0.50R -> break even at 66.7%
  target 0.25R -> break even at 80.0%

So an 80% win rate at a 0.25R target is exactly break-even BEFORE costs. There
is no version of this strategy where 80% is both reachable and profitable,
because the edge is +0.133R and buying 80% costs more than that.

The 80% target is hit almost exactly - 79.9% - and the strategy loses money

wins no longer cover the unchanged losses. Only the 1R target clears zero.

Halving the stop takes the model from +0.133R to -0.040R and TRIPLES the
drawdown to 31.3R. The pullback extreme is not an arbitrary choice - it is the
level the market has to respect for the setup to mean anything, and a stop
inside it gets hit by noise on the way to the same target.

Widening is a plateau, not an improvement. Every multiplier from 1.0 to 2.5
lands between +0.110R and +0.148R, and the whole spread is smaller than one
standard error (0.053R). The nominal best, 1.25x, reads +0.148R with a +0.045
lower bound, an 8.8R drawdown and in-sample +0.146 against out-of-sample +0.152
- but its +0.015R gain over the baseline is well inside the noise, and it was
picked from six candidates. Treat it as confirmation that the exact stop
placement does NOT matter much between 1x and 2.5x, which is good news for
trading it by hand, and not as a free upgrade.

The one consistent signal is that drawdown falls as the stop widens: 10.7R at
1x against 7.2-9.5R above it. Plausible - a wider stop breaks up losing runs -
and worth knowing, but it costs proportionally more points per trade, so the
percentage drawdown at fixed percentage risk is unchanged.

      0.50     False   349  57.6%  1.23   +0.080  -0.004    8.4

      0.75      True   349  60.2%  1.14   +0.058  -0.035   12.1

Taking half off at 0.3R and moving the rest to breakeven produces a 78.2% win
rate - the closest honest route to the 80% asked for - and cuts expectancy from
+0.133R to +0.026R, a loss of four fifths of the edge, while RAISING the
drawdown from 10.7R to 12.5R. Every partial variant has a lower bound below
zero. Scaling out without the breakeven move is less destructive but still
halves the edge.

--- CONCLUSION ---
An 80% win rate is reachable three different ways and all three destroy the
strategy. That is not a tuning failure; it is what a +0.133R edge permits. The
win rate is an output, not a dial.

The stop cannot be shortened. It is already at the only level with meaning, and
inside it the model is worse than a coin flip after costs.

What CAN be said: the stop placement is robust from 1x to 2.5x the pullback
extreme, so being a few points off by hand costs nothing measurable. That is
worth more to a manual trader than any of the win-rate constructions above.
