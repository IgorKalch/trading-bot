--- THREE THINGS THE GRID SAYS ---
1. TRAILING FROM ZERO IS DESTRUCTIVE ON BOTH LEGS. trail 0/0.5 takes the retest
   leg from +0.146R to -0.004R and the confluence leg from +0.066R to -0.002R.
   Moving to breakeven the moment a trade is green removes the entire edge on
   both, independently. On the confluence leg the win rate collapses to 20.6%.
   This is the most common piece of retail risk advice there is.

2. TRAILING'S ADVANTAGE IS ALWAYS OUTLIER-CARRIED. Every trailing variant on
   both legs has a NEGATIVE trim20 - expectancy after deleting the twenty best
   trades. Every fixed variant on both legs is positive. trail 1/1 reads +0.276R
   on the retest leg, the best single number in this project, and -0.053R once
   twenty trades of 349 are removed.

3. ON DAX THE NULL MODEL EXPOSES THE TRAILING VARIANTS. Confluence trailing on
   DAX looks better than fixed (+0.046R against +0.021R) but sits +0.3 sd above
   a same-days random-side null, where the fixed exits sit +3.5 to +3.9 sd. The
   trailing "edge" there is what a coin flip also earns.

The headline winner is trail 1/1 + fixed 1.5R at MAR 4.30. It inherits the
trim20 problem: its retest leg loses money without its best twenty trades, and
its yearly means run +0.44 / +3.06 / +2.03 / +0.80 - 2024 carries it.

The 2.0R version has the better MAR and the worse everything else: its
out-of-sample mean is a third below its in-sample, and its years swing by a
factor of seven. The 1.0R version agrees with itself out of sample to within
0.035R a month and its worst year is 57% of its best.
