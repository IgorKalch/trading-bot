===== THE IB CONFLUENCE FILTER: REPLICATED, THEN TRADED =====

The research (reports/research_open_types.txt) killed every mechanical open-type
classifier it found - kaiserfx, tradewrick, Marcn5_ - on determinism, baseline
or evidence. What survived was not a classifier but a set of base rates with
published samples, and one genuinely tradable conditioning rule.

--- THE RULE (nqstats, 2,571 NQ sessions, 2016-2025) ---
Both conditions are readable at 10:30:00 on closed bars. No hindsight.

  above_mid = close of the 10:25-10:30 bar > (IB_high + IB_low) / 2
  low_first = the IB low printed in an earlier 5-minute bar than the IB high
  bullish_confluence = above_mid AND low_first        (bearish is the mirror)

Claimed: bullish breaches the IB high 74.0% by noon and 84.0% by close, against
a 47.0% / 62.9% baseline.

--- IT REPLICATES ---
Measured on Dukascopy, a different vendor, a different period, and a second
instrument the source never tested:

                          claimed    our NQ     our DAX
  bullish, high by noon    +27.0pp   +29.4pp    +26.9pp
  bullish, high by close   +21.1pp   +23.7pp    +19.0pp
  bearish, low by noon     +28.1pp   +29.6pp    +29.0pp
  bearish, low by close    +23.1pp   +23.6pp    +19.9pp

  NQ absolute: baseline 48.1% / 62.4% -> confluence 77.4% / 86.2% (n=390)
  by year on NQ: 79%, 89%, 89%, 88% - stable

Four cells, two instruments, and all of them land within a few points of a
statistic published on 2,571 sessions of someone else's data. Nothing in this
project has replicated this cleanly before.

Only 1 NQ session in 905 and 9 DAX in 932 had both IB extremes inside the same
5-minute bar, so the 5-minute grid loses almost nothing against 1-minute.

--- BUT A PROBABILITY IS NOT AN EDGE ---
Traded mechanically: enter at the 10:30 open on confluence, stop at the far IB
extreme, fixed RR target, pessimistic same-bar resolution, futures-tick costs.

  market  stop   RR     n      WR    PF    expR    CI lo   DD_R
  NQ      IB    1.5   744   50.4%  1.17  +0.066   -0.002   12.6
  NQ      IB    1.0   744   52.8%  1.10  +0.038   -0.022   12.6
  NQ      mid   2.0   744   38.4%  1.06  +0.039   -0.059   23.5
  DAX     IB    1.5   743   44.5%  1.04  +0.021   -0.057   33.5
  DAX     mid   any   743      -   <0.9  negative      -   >100

NQ at 1.5R is +0.066R with its interval touching zero at -0.002. Against a null
model that takes the SAME days and picks the side at random, which returns
-0.108R, the real rule sits 4.7 standard deviations above. On DAX, +0.021R
against a -0.231R null, 4.2 sd above.

So the direction signal is real and large. The trade built on it is not, yet,
because entering at 10:30 with the stop at the far side of the Initial Balance
makes 1R the whole IB width - the most expensive possible way to express a
directional view.

--- WHERE IT SITS ---
  incumbent NQ retest    349 trades   +0.146R   DD 10.3R   1.16R/mo   MAR 1.35
  IB confluence, NQ      744 trades   +0.066R   DD 12.6R   1.12R/mo   MAR 1.06

Nearly the same monthly R from twice the trades at half the edge. It does not
beat the incumbent, but it is the first candidate that trades at a completely
different time of day (10:30 against 09:30-10:00) off a signal verified against
an independent published sample.

--- WHAT TO TRY NEXT, IN ORDER ---
1. A cheaper expression of the same view: enter on the IB-high break rather than
   at 10:30, with the stop at a swing low or the IB midpoint, so 1R is a
   fraction of the IB rather than all of it.
2. Target the measured move (100% of IB range beyond the boundary), which the
   research documents at 12.8% up / 15.9% down unconditionally on NQ, instead of
   a fixed RR.
3. The C-period rule: a 10:55-11:00 close outside the IB more than doubles the
   100%-extension rate (NQ 33.2% up against 12.8%). Must be measured in the
   tradable variant that scans only AFTER the signal bar closes - the published
   figure may include the move that created the signal.
4. Correlation with the retest model. Different hour, different trigger; if it
   is low, the two together are worth more than either.
