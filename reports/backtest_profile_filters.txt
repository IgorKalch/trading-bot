===== TEN MARKET PROFILE FILTERS, THIRTY CELLS, ONE DIAGNOSTIC =====

The brief: raise profitability, or cut drawdown by rejecting trades that lose.
So the metric is not expectancy. Expectancy rises automatically whenever a
filter trades less and the remaining sample happens to be luckier. The two
numbers that decide a filter are:

  %tr vs %loss   what fraction of TRADES it keeps against what fraction of the
                 total LOSSES it keeps. A filter that keeps 40% of trades and
                 40% of the losses has separated nothing - it shrank the sample.
  MAR            annualised return over maximum drawdown. This holds the
                 drawdown constant, so a rule cannot look good by trading less.

Ten hypotheses, PRE-SPECIFIED from Market Profile theory rather than scanned,
each with a stated reason to exist, against three live models on NQ. Thirty
cells; at 5% roughly 1.5 false positives are expected by chance.

Infrastructure: tools/mprofile.py builds a real TPO profile from one-minute
highs and lows - POC, value area, VAH, VAL, single prints, poor highs and lows,
naked POCs, balance and trend days. Bin height is the prior 14-day range over
120, never a fixed number of points, so the histogram cannot become a date
filter as the index level grows. Only features complete BEFORE the session's
first tick are used as filters: yesterday's profile, and where today opened
relative to it.

===== THE DIAGNOSTIC, AND IT IS THE WHOLE ANSWER =====

In 29 of 30 cells, %loss lands within a few points of %tr. Read the two columns
side by side: 67/66, 39/38, 46/47, 33/33, 64/64, 83/84, 37/35, 84/84, 45/44,
39/39. These filters do not separate winners from losers. They shrink the
sample, and expectancy then moves around by the amount sampling noise permits.

That is not a subtle statistical failure - it is the filters having no relation
to the outcome. And it is visible without any test of significance.

The one exception, the only cell in thirty where the rejected trades actually
LOST money: the confluence model with yesterday's range under 1.0 ATR.
Expectancy +0.0760 -> +0.1209, lower bound +0.0346, and R/mo essentially
unchanged at +1.273 -> +1.299 because it keeps the trades that matter. Its MAR
is 1.77 against 1.82 unfiltered, so it does not clear the bar either, but it is
the only lead in the file and it is the only rule whose mechanism is not
Market-Profile-shaped: it is a volatility-regime filter wearing profile clothes.

===== WHY THE TWO APPARENT WINNERS ARE NOT WINNERS =====

Two cells beat unfiltered MAR on the retest model, and both fail on inspection.

NAKED POC (MAR 1.65 -> 2.40). It keeps 37% of trades and 35% of the losses - no
separation. Its whole gain is a drawdown falling from 8.3R to 3.3R, and a
maximum drawdown measured on 119 trades is one of the noisiest statistics in
this project. R/mo falls from +1.138 to +0.660, and the trades it rejects
averaged +0.1174R - it is discarding profit. Decisively: in-sample +0.0962
against out-of-sample +0.3890. An effect that lives almost entirely in the
second half of the data is a sampling artefact, not a rule.

NOT A TREND DAY (MAR 1.65 -> 2.05). Keeps 83% of trades and 84% of the losses.
The trades it REJECTS averaged +0.1954R - better than the ones it keeps. It
wins on MAR purely because removing a sixth of the trades happened to clip the
drawdown path. Rejecting your best trades is not a filter.

===== WHAT THIS SETTLES =====

The Market Profile filter family does not work on these models. Not "is not
significant" - does not separate winners from losers at all, in thirty
pre-specified cells, on three models with independent entry logic.

This agrees with what the open-type measurement already said: the classification
carries no information. It now extends to the rest of the tradition - value area
location, the POC, naked POCs, balance and trend days. The one variable that
showed anything was yesterday's RANGE, which is volatility, not profile.

Practical consequence: leave all three models unfiltered. The drive model's
12.1R drawdown is not reducible this way. The way to cut portfolio drawdown
remains what the scaling work already established - add uncorrelated legs, which
took the three-leg portfolio to a 3.80R drawdown without discarding a single
trade.

===== HONEST LIMITS =====
- Thirty cells, so about 1.5 chance positives were expected. Two cells beat the
  unfiltered MAR, which is exactly the expected count, and both fail inspection.
  That is the correct reading, not "two filters work".
- Thresholds inside the hypotheses (0.25 ATR for the gap, 1.0 ATR for the prior
  range, 0.15-0.45 ATR for the window, 1.0 ATR for the naked POC distance) were
  each chosen once, before running, and not scanned. Scanning them would find
  better-looking cells and none of them would mean anything.
- Balance and trend day definitions are mine, not Dalton's - a trend day here is
  a range over 1.1 ATR closing in the outer 20%, a balance day a value area
  holding over 55% of a range under 1.0 ATR. Both land at plausible frequencies
  (16% and 11% of NQ sessions), but a different definition would give different
  numbers. Given that the balance-day cell has 100 trades and the widest
  confidence interval in the table, this is not where the answer hinges.
- NQ only. The models are only positive on NQ, so filtering them elsewhere would
  be filtering noise.
