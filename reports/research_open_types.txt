### Bar grid and Initial Balance definition (canonical 09:30-10:30 ET first hour)
SOURCE: https://tradingstats.net/initial-balance-breakout-statistics/
DEFINITION: VERIFIED VERBATIM: "The Initial Balance (IB) is the price range established during the first hour of Regular Trading Hours - from 9:30 AM to 10:30 AM Eastern Time. It consists of two 30-minute TPO periods: the A-period (9:30-10:00) and the B-period (10:00-10:30)." nqstats states the same window: "The Initial Balance (IB) is defined as the 09:30 - 10:30 ET range (the first hour of the NY equity session)." steady-turtle: "the first 60 minutes of RTH, 09:30 to 10:30 ET". Every quantitative source found uses this identical window; none uses a different IB length for index futures.
MECHANICAL: THIS ITEM DEFINES THE BAR GRID USED BY EVERY ITEM BELOW. Load 5-minute OHLC bars stamped by bar OPEN time, timezone America/New_York, DST-aware. Keep only RTH bars with open timestamps 09:30 through 15:55 inclusive = exactly 78 bars per session. Index them b1..b78 (b1 opens 09:30, b12 opens 10:25, b13 opens 10:30, b18 opens 10:55, b30 opens 11:55, b78 opens 15:55). Drop any session not containing all 78 bars (half-days, feed outages) and report the dropped count. Round all prices to the 0.25 tick grid before any comparison. THEN: IB bars = b1..b12, covering 09:30:00.000 to 10:29:59.999. IB_high = max(high of b1..b12). IB_low = min(low of b1..b12). IB_range = IB_high - IB_low. IB_mid = (IB_high + IB_low) / 2. Post-IB bars = b13..b78. session_close = close of b78. day_high = max(high of b1..b78). day_low = min(low of b1..b78). CLOSED-BAR LAW: a value computed from bar bk may be acted on only from bk+1 onward. IB_high/IB_low/IB_range are first available at the open of b13 (10:30:00), which is where every rule below starts, so no rule below has lookahead. RESOLUTION NOTE (state this in any writeup): IB_high and IB_low are bit-identical on 5-minute and 1-minute bars because both are extrema over the same 60-minute window, so every touch / breach / extension statistic below is resolution-invariant and reproduces the published 1-minute figures exactly. Five-minute bars lose only (a) the intrabar ordering of two events inside one 5-minute bar and (b) closes taken at non-5-minute boundaries. Every rule below that depends on either is given an explicit deterministic tie-break.
EVIDENCE: Window identical across all sources with real samples: tradingstats.net (ES 2,686 + NQ 2,833 days, 5 Jan 2015 - 29 Dec 2025), nqstats.com (2,571 NQ days, 2016-2025), noesisanalytics.io (~1,337 ES sessions, no date range published), steady-turtle.com (1,586 NQ sessions, five years). No source uses a different IB length for index futures. VERIFICATION: all four quotes confirmed by direct fetch.

### IB extension probability to 100% and 200% of IB range (ES and NQ)
SOURCE: https://tradingstats.net/initial-balance-breakout-statistics/
DEFINITION: Probability price travels a full IB range (100% extension) or two IB ranges (200%) beyond the IB boundary, measured "By Close". ES 100%: Upside 18.8%, Downside 20.5%. NQ 100%: Upside 12.8%, Downside 15.9%. ES 200%: Upside 4.4%, Downside 7.1%. NQ 200%: Upside 2.1%, Downside 4.1%.
MECHANICAL: Using the b1..b78 grid of item 1. Skip any session where IB_range <= 0 (guard only; cannot occur on index futures). up_ext = (max(high of b13..b78) - IB_high) / IB_range. dn_ext = (IB_low - min(low of b13..b78)) / IB_range. Both may be negative if that side never traded past the boundary; do not clamp. hit_100_up = (up_ext >= 1.00). hit_200_up = (up_ext >= 2.00). hit_100_dn = (dn_ext >= 1.00). hit_200_dn = (dn_ext >= 2.00). Up and down are evaluated independently on every session, so a double-breakout day can set both flags; the up rate and the down rate are NOT mutually exclusive and MUST NOT be summed to produce an 'either' rate - to get an 'either' rate compute max(up_ext, dn_ext) >= 1.00 as a separate variable. Denominator for every rate = all sessions surviving the 78-bar filter, NOT just breakout sessions. Wicks count; no close confirmation; no tick buffer. Resolution-invariant: identical on 1-minute bars.
EVIDENCE: n = 2,686 ES / 2,833 NQ days, 2015-2025. ES 100% ext 18.8% up / 20.5% down; NQ 12.8% / 15.9%. ES 200% 4.4% / 7.1%; NQ 2.1% / 4.1%. All eight figures confirmed verbatim by fetch. Downside exceeds upside at every level on both instruments - the asymmetry is consistent across four independent cells, not noise. Sub-sample counts for these cells are NOT published.

### First IB break failure rate (~34% on both ES and NQ) - AMBIGUITY NOW RESOLVED
SOURCE: https://tradingstats.net/initial-balance-breakout-statistics/
DEFINITION: VERIFIED VERBATIM: "First break failed (close inside IB): ES 34.0%, NQ 34.8%", with the definition "False IB breakout occurs when price breaks beyond the IB high or IB low but closes back inside the IB range by end of day."
MECHANICAL: Using the b1..b78 grid of item 1. Step 1 - find k = the smallest index in 13..78 such that (high of bk > IB_high) OR (low of bk < IB_low). If no such k, the session has no first break and is excluded from this item's denominator. Step 2 - assign the direction of bar bk deterministically (needed because one 5-minute bar can pierce both sides, which 1-minute bars would have ordered): IF (high of bk > IB_high) AND NOT (low of bk < IB_low) THEN dir = up. ELSE IF (low of bk < IB_low) AND NOT (high of bk > IB_high) THEN dir = dn. ELSE (both pierced in bar bk): IF close of bk > IB_high THEN dir = up; ELSE IF close of bk < IB_low THEN dir = dn; ELSE IF (high of bk - IB_high) > (IB_low - low of bk) THEN dir = up; ELSE IF (high of bk - IB_high) < (IB_low - low of bk) THEN dir = dn; ELSE dir = up (exact-tie fallback, deterministic). Count and report how many sessions hit the both-pierced branch - that count is the entire 5-minute-vs-1-minute divergence for this item. Step 3 - failed = (IB_low <= session_close <= IB_high), inclusive on both bounds, where session_close = close of b78. Report failure rate as failed_count / first_break_count. The 'end of day' reading is now CONFIRMED by the source's own words, so the earlier caveat about a possible 'first close back inside' reading is withdrawn.
EVIDENCE: n = 2,686 ES / 2,833 NQ days, 2015-2025. ES 34.0%, NQ 34.8%. Definition confirmed verbatim as end-of-day, removing the prior ambiguity. The near-identical figure across two instruments with different volatility profiles argues the measurement is real rather than curve-fit. Raw counts for the failure cells NOT published.

SOURCE: https://tradingstats.net/initial-balance-breakout-statistics/
DEFINITION: VERIFIED: tiers "Narrow: < 0.5x ATR, Normal: 0.5-1.0x, Wide: 1.0-1.5x, Extreme: >1.5x" with "ATR calculation: 14-period ATR on daily RTH bars". MECHANICAL: Using the b1..b78 grid of item 1. Step 1 - build one daily RTH bar per session from the 5-minute bars: D_high = max(high b1..b78), D_low = min(low b1..b78), D_close = close of b78. Step 2 - true range for session t: TR_t = max(D_high_t - D_low_t, abs(D_high_t - D_close_(t-1)), abs(D_low_t - D_close_(t-1))). Step 3 - ATR14_t = arithmetic mean (simple, not Wilder) of the 14 values TR_(t-14) through TR_(t-1) - i.e. fourteen COMPLETED prior sessions, strictly excluding session t, which removes all lookahead. Sessions with fewer than 15 prior sessions available have no ATR14 and are excluded; report the excluded count. Wilder smoothing is an equally defensible reading of '14-period ATR' and reassigns a minority of days across tier boundaries - pick the simple mean, state it, and never swap mid-study. Step 4 - tier assignment, boundaries closed on the left: narrow if IB_range < 0.50 * ATR14_t; normal if 0.50 * ATR14_t <= IB_range < 1.00 * ATR14_t; wide if 1.00 * ATR14_t <= IB_range < 1.50 * ATR14_t; extreme if IB_range >= 1.50 * ATR14_t. Step 5 - within each tier compute breakout / double / none exactly per item 2, and THREE extension medians rather than one: (a) median_ext_IBmult = median of max(up_ext, dn_ext) from item 3, expressed as a percentage of IB_range - this is the published metric; (b) median_ext_points = median of max(max(high b13..b78) - IB_high, IB_low - min(low b13..b78)) in index points; (c) median_ext_ATR = metric (b) divided by ATR14_t. HARD RULES: (1) Report all three medians side by side. The published 74.8% vs 39.6% is normalised BY IB_range, so the small denominator mechanically inflates the narrow tier - 74.8% of a 6-point IB is 4.5 points while 39.6% of a 25-point IB is 9.9 points. The narrow tier extends further in IB multiples and LESS in absolute points. Metrics (b) and (c) are where the claimed edge either survives or dissolves, and metric (b) is the one a stop-loss actually pays. (2) Do not report, fit, or trade any tier cell with n < 100. That rule deletes ES Extreme (n=6) and NQ Extreme (n=13) outright.
EVIDENCE: n = 2,686 ES / 2,833 NQ days, 2015-2025. All 40 table cells and both the tier thresholds and the ATR definition confirmed verbatim by fetch. Narrow tier is the majority of days: 1,779/2,686 = 66.2% of ES days, 1,584/2,833 = 55.9% of NQ days. Monotonic across all four tiers on both instruments for breakout rate, double-break rate and median extension. Tier counts sum to 2,674 (ES) and 2,821 (NQ) against parent samples of 2,686 and 2,833 - a shortfall of exactly 12 sessions on each instrument, which matches the ATR14 warm-up exclusion (first 14-15 sessions) almost exactly and is therefore explained rather than unreconciled.

Use it as the first calibration test of the bar pipeline before running anything else - if item 12 does not reproduce, no other item's replication can be trusted.
EVIDENCE: n = 3,147 NQ + 3,064 ES = 6,211 RTH sessions, 17 Feb 2014 - 1 May 2026 (12 years), 1-minute bars. All twelve percentages plus both median distances confirmed verbatim. The 30M-to-60M step costs 8.3pp (NQ) and 8.6pp (ES) of outside-close probability while doubling the wait - a direct measurement of what using the IB instead of a 30-minute OR costs. ES closes outside more often than NQ at every window by a stable 1.5-2.1pp. Minor internal tension worth noting: the "27-30%" base-rate sentence matches the 30M containment row (29.4% / 27.2%) but not the 60M row (37.7% / 35.8%), so read it as a statement about the 30-minute OR specifically, not about all three windows.

## 1. Bar grid, Initial Balance, and the four open types

### 1.1 Bar grid (defines every rule below)

Load 5-minute OHLC bars stamped by **bar OPEN time**, converted to the instrument's local exchange timezone, DST-aware:

- **NQ (NDXUSD)** — America/New_York. RTH bars with open stamps 09:30 … 15:55 = **78 bars**, indexed b1..b78. b1 opens 09:30, b6 opens 09:55, b12 opens 10:25, b13 opens 10:30, b78 opens 15:55.
- **FDAX (DEUIDXEUR)** — Europe/Berlin. Xetra bars with open stamps 09:00 … 17:25 = **102 bars**, indexed b1..b102. b1 opens 09:00, b6 opens 09:25, b12 opens 09:55, b13 opens 10:00.

Drop any session that does not contain the full bar count (half-days, feed outages) and **report the dropped count**. Round every price to the instrument's tick grid before any comparison: **NQ 0.25, DAX 0.50** (the futures grids; the Dukascopy CFDs quote finer, so this is a deliberate quantisation that makes touch/breach tests reproducible against published futures statistics).

**Closed-bar law.** A value computed from bar bk may be acted on only from the open of b(k+1) onward. No exceptions, no intrabar logic.

### 1.2 Initial Balance

IB bars = **b1..b12** (the first 60 minutes: 09:30–10:29:59 ET / 09:00–09:59:59 Berlin).

```
IB_high  = max(high b1..b12)
IB_low   = min(low  b1..b12)
IB_range = IB_high - IB_low
IB_mid   = (IB_high + IB_low) / 2
```

First available at the **open of b13**. Every entry rule below starts there, so no rule has lookahead.

Why 60 minutes and not 30: every quantitative source that publishes IB statistics for index futures uses the first hour (09:30–10:30 ET, two 30-minute TPO periods) without exception, the authors say "Initial Balance" rather than "opening range", and this project's own prior measurement (`reports/backtest_ib_open_types.txt`) used the first hour, so results stay comparable. Note the cost of the choice, measured elsewhere: extending 30m → 60m buys about 8pp of close-inside containment while halving the tradeable window.

Note: `IB_high`/`IB_low` are bit-identical on 1-minute and 5-minute bars (both are extrema over the same 60 minutes), so nothing in the IB layer is resolution-dependent.

### 1.3 Scale normaliser (pinned, and why)

```
D_high_t = max(high b1..bN), D_low_t = min(low b1..bN), D_close_t = close(bN)   # N = 78 or 102
TR_t     = max(D_high_t - D_low_t, |D_high_t - D_close_(t-1)|, |D_low_t - D_close_(t-1)|)
ATR14_t  = arithmetic mean of TR_(t-14) .. TR_(t-1)          # simple mean, NOT Wilder
```

Fourteen **completed prior** sessions, strictly excluding session t. Sessions with fewer than 14 prior sessions are excluded; report the count.

Every threshold below is expressed as a fraction of `ATR14`, never in absolute points. This is not cosmetic: `Додаток И` of this project records an absolute-points IB-width filter that read PF 1.50 and turned out to be a **date filter** (share of NQ days with IB > 120 pts ran 34.8% in 2023 → 92.9% in 2026 as the index went 12,000 → 29,000). Any point-denominated threshold on this data set is a disguised regime filter. Simple mean over Wilder is a coin-flip reading of "14-period ATR"; pick simple, state it, never swap mid-study.

### 1.4 The classification window and its primitives

The open type is decided on the **first 30 minutes**, bars b1..b6, and emitted exactly once at the close of b6 (i.e. usable from the open of b7, long before b13 where trading is armed).

```
O    = open(b1)
P1H  = max(high b1..b6)      i_high = SMALLEST i in 1..6 with high(bi) == P1H
P1L  = min(low  b1..b6)      i_low  = SMALLEST i in 1..6 with low(bi)  == P1L
P1C  = close(b6)
P1R  = P1H - P1L
```

Ties always break to the **earliest** bar. This matters: on a 0.25 tick grid, `i_high`/`i_low` ties occur on 0.5–8% of sessions depending on instrument, and an unpinned `argmin` produces disagreeing label sets.

**Degeneracy guard.** If `P1R < 4 ticks`, or `ATR14_t` is unavailable or ≤ 0, or `P1C == O`, the session is **NA** — no label, no trade. Report the NA count broken out by cause. (`P1C == O` is the drive-direction tie; it is rare and must not be silently resolved.)

```
dir     = +1 if P1C > O else -1
favour  = (P1H - O) if dir == +1 else (O - P1L)
adverse = (O - P1L) if dir == +1 else (P1H - O)
crossed = (min(low  b2..b6) <= O) if dir == +1 else (max(high b2..b6) >= O)
i_fav   = i_high if dir == +1 else i_low
i_adv   = i_low  if dir == +1 else i_high
k_drive = 0.25 * ATR14_t
k_probe = 0.10 * ATR14_t
```

**`crossed` is measured over b2..b6, excluding b1.** By the definition of an OHLC bar, `low(b1) <= open(b1) = O` and `high(b1) >= O` always, so a "never traded back through the open" test that includes b1 is **identically false** and the Open Drive class can never fire. This is not hypothetical — it is the exact defect that made one published mechanisation of this taxonomy produce zero OD days on 921 sessions. Excluding b1 means "no return to the open after the first five minutes", which is the only reading that both fires and matches the intent.

### 1.5 The four open types (first match wins, exactly one label per session)

```
1. OA   if favour < k_drive
2. OD   if (not crossed) and (adverse < k_probe)
3. ORR  if (adverse >= k_drive) and (i_adv < i_fav)
4. OTD  otherwise
```

All comparisons strict where written strict. Priority order is part of the definition; OA is tested first so a directionless morning can never be relabelled by a coincidental probe, and OTD is the residual so the partition is exhaustive by construction.

Plain-language check on each:

- **OA (Open Auction)** — the first 30 minutes resolved less than a quarter of an ATR in the eventual direction. No conviction. Not traded.
- **OD (Open Drive)** — went one way from the open, never came back through the open after b1, and gave back less than 0.10 ATR. Not traded (the authors trade only OTD and ORR).
- **ORR (Open Rejection Reverse)** — a real excursion of at least 0.25 ATR **against** the eventual direction, printed **before** the favourable extreme, then reversed through the open. The `i_adv < i_fav` clause is an addition to the published wording and it is load-bearing: without it, a session that rallied, then sold off, then closed above the open is labelled ORR-UP, which is the wrong causal shape. On measured data 2–5% of ORR labels have this defect. It is a one-line deterministic fix; take it.
- **OTD (Open Test Drive)** — everything else: a shallow probe (between 0.10 and 0.25 ATR against, or a deeper one that crossed the open) followed by a drive.

**The "known reference level" condition is deliberately NOT in the primary classifier.** Dalton's OTD requires the probe to test a pre-known reference (PDH/PDL, prior-day VAH/VAL, overnight H/L). Adding it requires a value-area recipe (profile type, period count, grid size, POC tie-break, expansion rule) whose two defensible readings flip up to 45% of labels — and when it was measured on 929 NQ / 932 DAX sessions it moved every outcome by less than noise (all p > 0.2). Implement it instead as a **separate boolean column** `ref_touch` (probe extreme within 0.05·ATR14 of any of PDH, PDL, ON_high, ON_low, computed from the previous completed session and the overnight window), report the conditional numbers with and without it, and do not let it into the trade rule unless it demonstrably moves something.

### 1.6 Pre-registered threshold sweep

`k_probe ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15}` × `k_drive ∈ {0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35}` × classification window `∈ {6 bars}`. Twelve cells, declared **before** running, reported as a full table, with the 0.10/0.25 cell as the pre-registered primary. Rationale: the same taxonomy under equally defensible thresholds produces OTD counts spanning a factor of 2.2–2.5, so a single unswept threshold is a hidden free parameter. Any conclusion that holds only in one cell is noise. Apply a Bonferroni-style discount: a nominal p of 0.05 in this family means nothing below p ≈ 0.004.

## 2. The 5m BOS entry

Everything below runs on the same b-index grid, closed bars only.

### 2.1 Swing points (fractals)

A **swing high** at index k requires, with L = R = 2:

```
high(bk) > high(bk-1) and high(bk) > high(bk-2) and high(bk) > high(bk+1) and high(bk) > high(bk+2)
```

All four comparisons strict — equal highs produce no swing, which is the deterministic resolution of the flat-top case. Swing low is the mirror with `low` and strict `<`. A bar may be both.

A swing at index k is **confirmed at the close of b(k+2)** and may be acted on **from the open of b(k+3)**. This two-bar confirmation lag is where most published BOS backtests leak: a swing drawn on a chart is visible instantly, but a 2/2 fractal is not knowable until two bars later. Encode the lag explicitly.

L = R = 2 is the default because it is the standard 5-bar fractal in the "5m BOS" material this setup comes from, and on M5 it yields roughly one swing per 5–6 bars — frequent enough to give entries inside the armed window, coarse enough that the stop is not one tick wide. Sweep `L = R ∈ {1, 2, 3}` once, as a plateau check, not as a search.

State: `SH_active` = the most recently confirmed, not-yet-broken swing high; `SL_active` = mirror. A newer confirmation replaces an older one. A broken swing is consumed.

### 2.2 What counts as a break

For a long (`dir == +1`), at the close of bar bj with `j >= 13` and `bj` confirmed-usable:

```
BOS_long  = close(bj) >= SH_active + 1 tick
BOS_short = close(bj) <= SL_active - 1 tick
```

**Close-based, not wick-based**, and one full tick beyond. Reasons: this repo's incumbent retest model already uses body-close confirmation (`confirmation.require_body_close: true`), a wick break on M5 is unstable against the 0.25/0.50 tick grid, and a close-based rule removes every intrabar-ordering ambiguity that 5-minute resolution would otherwise create.

Side gating (the authors' actual claim):

- Trade only sessions labelled **OTD or ORR**. OD, OA and NA sessions are skipped entirely.
- Trade only in the direction `dir` of that label.

Two declared variants, both measured:

- **V1 (BOS only)** — the BOS is taken wherever it occurs in the armed window.
- **V2 (BOS after IB extension)** — the BOS is taken only after price has already traded beyond the IB boundary on the `dir` side (`high > IB_high` for a long, `low < IB_low` for a short) at some bar in b13..bj. V2 is the literal "Initial Balance + open type" reading; V1 isolates the entry model from the IB half.

### 2.3 Window, frequency, entry price

- Armed from the **open of b13** (10:30 ET / 10:00 Berlin).
- No new entry after **14:00 ET** (NQ) / **15:00 Berlin** (DAX — before the 15:30 US-open volatility regime, which is a different data-generating process).
- Force flat at **15:50 ET** / **17:20 Berlin** (matches `session.flat_time` in both configs).
- At most **one open position at a time**, at most **2 entries per session**, both in the same `dir`. No re-entry on the bar that closed a position.
- Entry fills at the **open of b(j+1)** through the existing engine fill model (`backtest.spread_points`, `backtest.slippage_points`, `pessimistic_same_bar: true`) so numbers stay comparable with every other report in `reports/`.

### 2.4 Stop

For a long:

```
k_SH      = the bar index at which SH_active printed
stop_ref  = min(low of bars b(k_SH) .. bj inclusive)
stop      = stop_ref - max(sl_buffer_points, 1 tick)     # sl_buffer_points = 2.0 in both configs
R_points  = entry_price - stop
```

Mirror for a short. `stop_ref` always exists, so there is no undefined branch. Declared variant **V-S2**: stop at the most recent confirmed swing low instead of the running minimum — measure it, do not choose it by eye.

Two geometry guards, both reported as diagnostics before either is switched on:

- Skip if `R_points > 1.00 * IB_range` — at that size the trade is strictly worse than a plain IB breakout with the stop at the far IB boundary, and the setup has no reason to exist.
- Skip if `cost_points / R_points > 0.06` (NQ: R < 39 pts; DAX: R < 37.5 pts) — see §5, step 1.

### 2.5 Target

Fixed RR off `R_points`: primary **1:1**, also reported at **1:1.5** and **1:2** — exactly the three the authors publish, so the comparison is like-for-like. Time exit at flat time counts as a partial-R outcome, recorded with `exit_reason = time`. Same-bar stop-and-target ambiguity resolves to the **stop** (`pessimistic_same_bar: true`).

### 2.6 Per-trade log (required for every run)

`date, instrument, class, dir, ref_touch, ATR14, IB_range, IB_range/ATR14, entry_bar_index, R_points, cost/R, exit_reason, result_R`. Section 4 is untestable without these columns.

## 3. The null hypothesis and how to measure it

### 3.1 What must be beaten, stated as three separate nulls

The setup makes three chained claims, and each has its own null. Test them separately or the result is uninterpretable.

**Claim A — "OTD/ORR days extend; other types do not."**
Null A: the unconditional rate of the outcome, on the same sessions, with no classification. Do **not** use "the IB extends" as the outcome — that is 96.7% on NQ and 97.2% on DAX and cannot be beaten. The outcome variable is the **CLEAN day** already defined in `tools/opentype.py`: the IB extends in the drive direction and the other side is never taken. Its measured baseline is **NQ 49.3%, DAX 43.1%**. OTD+ORR must beat that, on both instruments, in the same direction, across years.
Additional null A2 (diffusion): 400 replicates per session in which the post-IB 5-minute log returns are shuffled within the session, preserving that session's IB, its bar-range distribution and its intraday volatility profile, destroying only sequence. Report the CLEAN rate by class against this shuffled null. A conditional rate that a shuffled session reproduces is geometry, not information.

**Claim B — "the 5m BOS entry has an edge."**
Null B: the project's standard random-entry null with matched stops. For each real trade, generate synthetic trades on the **same session**, with the **same R_points**, the **same target RR**, the **same exit machinery**, the **same costs**, and an entry bar drawn uniformly from the armed window `[b13 … cutoff]`.
 - **B1 (unconditional)**: side drawn 50/50. Tests whether the entry carries anything at all.
 - **B2 (direction-matched, paired)**: side fixed to the real trade's `dir`, same day, same R. This is the decisive one — it removes the day filter, the drift, and the direction, leaving only the *timing and structure* of the BOS. Every prior adversarial test of this taxonomy that looked positive collapsed at exactly this step.
Run ≥ 12 seeds (project precedent: the incumbent retest model reports null −0.028R, sd 0.027, real 4.6 sd above). Report null mean expR, sd across seeds, and `z = (real − null_mean) / sd_null`.

**Claim C — "the classification adds something the entry does not already have."**
Null C: run the **identical BOS strategy on the rejected days** (OD, OA). If OTD/ORR days do not beat rejected days by a margin larger than the sampling error, the classification is decoration and only the entry survives. This is the cheapest and most conclusive test in the whole document, and it is the one the authors never ran.

### 3.2 Acceptance criteria, pre-registered before the first run

All must hold; any single failure kills the setup:

1. `z >= 3.0` against Null B2 (not 2.0 — this project has already examined 594 cells, and the multiple-testing debt is real).
2. 95% CI lower bound on expR > 0.
3. Same sign on **both** NQ and DAX.
4. Positive in at least 3 of 4 calendar years, with no single year carrying > 60% of total R.
5. `median(cost/R) <= 0.06`.
6. OTD+ORR beats the CLEAN baseline by ≥ 5pp with the same sign in ≥ 10 of the 12 pre-registered threshold cells.

### 3.3 Power, computed up front

Per-trade sd at fixed 1R is ≈ 1.0R. To detect `+0.10R` at z = 3 needs n ≈ 900 trades; at ~0.5–1.0 trades per qualifying session and roughly 300–450 qualifying sessions per instrument (OTD+ORR should be well under half of 943), a single instrument yields perhaps 150–400 trades — enough to detect **+0.15R to +0.25R**, not enough for +0.10R. That is fine for this specific test, because the authors claim **+0.528R** at 1:1 (76.4% win rate), which would be visible at n = 50 with z > 3. The experiment is well powered against the claim actually being made; it is underpowered against the claim being marginally true. Say so in the report.

### 3.4 Breakeven arithmetic to quote alongside every win rate

At fixed 1:1 with cost fraction c: `expR = 2·WR − 1 − c`, so breakeven `WR = (1 + c)/2`. At the likely NQ cost/R of 6%, breakeven is **53.0%**; at DAX 6%, the same. The measured mechanical version of this setup's own IB half came in at **51.6%** on DAX — i.e. below breakeven — against the authors' claimed 72%.

## 4. The three most likely ways this setup is fooling its authors

Ranked by expected size of the distortion.

**1. Discretionary stop placement — worth roughly the entire claimed edge.**
The authors' own slide says the first approach uses "FLEXIBLE stop placement depending on context". A stop chosen after seeing the context is a stop chosen where it will not be hit; because R is the denominator of every reported ratio, shrinking R after the fact manufactures a 1:1 win rate directly. This is not speculation: this project already mechanised the same premise on the same instrument and measured **51.6% against the claimed 72% — a twenty-point gap** on FDAX. The two facts that fit together are (a) their win rate is 20pp above mechanical and (b) their stop rule is not a rule. Test: the specification above fixes R by rule; if the mechanical version lands near 52% again, the gap is the stop, and no amount of open-type filtering recovers it. Diagnostic to publish: the full distribution of `R_points`, and what win rate a 30% reduction in median R would produce at the same trade set.

**2. Eye-classified open types leak the outcome.**
The label is assigned by looking at a chart on which the rest of the session is already drawn. "Was this an OTD?" and "did it drive?" are answered from the same picture, so the classification is confirmed by the very extension it is supposed to predict. The size of the leak is measurable and has been measured: mechanising this taxonomy moves the extension rate by **1.6 points** (97.3% vs 95.7% on NQ), while their eye-assigned version appears to move the win rate by 20 points. That gap is the leak. A second, compounding mechanism: the label set is not stable under defensible thresholds — OTD counts vary by a factor of 2.2–2.5 across probe-depth and bar-count choices no source pins — so a human classifier is effectively re-choosing the threshold on every chart, which is per-day curve fitting. Test: Null C (§3.1). If the rejected days trade as well as the accepted ones, the whole taxonomy is post-hoc.

**3. One year per instrument, ~85–196 trades, no split, and the filter was chosen after seeing the results.**
They report FDAX 2024 (196 positions, of which 85 are the open-type variant) and one NQ year. There is no out-of-sample split, no per-year stability, no baseline stated for the "other types have a low chance of extension" premise — a premise this project measured as false, since **all** types extend 96.7–97.2% of the time. And the choice to trade 2 of the 4 types is itself a selection made after observing four candidate filters, which is a 4-cell search reported as a hypothesis. At n = 85, one standard error on a win rate is 5.4pp, so a 76.4% reading has a wide honest interval even before the selection is priced in; 2024 was also a strongly trending year on both instruments, which flatters any long-biased drive filter. Test: per-year breakout, in-sample/out-of-sample split, and long/short split, exactly as `reports/backtest_instruments.txt` section D does for the incumbent.

**Runner-up (4th), stated because it may dominate everything above:** costs are absent from their arithmetic. A 5m BOS stop is far tighter than an IB-width stop, so cost/R rises sharply — see step 1 below. If median cost/R lands near 10%, breakeven at 1:1 moves to 55% and their entire margin over a coin flip is friction.

## 5. Implementation order, ranked by information per unit of work

**Step 1 — Cost/R feasibility from geometry alone. (half a day; highest information per unit of work by a wide margin.)**
Build the swing/BOS geometry in `tools/ib_bos.py` and, on all 943 sessions per instrument, emit the distribution of `R_points` for every BOS that *would* have fired — with no P&L, no exits, no classification. Then compute `cost_points / R_points` using the measured 2.34 (NQ) and 2.25 (DAX). Cost/R is this project's known binding constraint (incumbent models: NQ 1.4%, DAX 4.5%, ES 6.1%). If the median lands above ~8%, the setup is dead on arithmetic and no further work is justified. This is one pass over the data and it can end the project on day one.

**Step 2 — The classification's own claim, still with no trading. (half a day; reuses existing code.)**
Extend `tools/opentype.py` to the classifier of §1.5 (add the `i_adv < i_fav` ORR fix, the b2..b6 `crossed` window, the NA guards, the 12-cell threshold sweep, and the `ref_touch` column). Report CLEAN-day rate by class against the 49.3% / 43.1% baseline, plus Null A2 (shuffled post-IB returns). If OTD+ORR does not clear the baseline by ≥ 5pp with a stable sign across both instruments and all years, the day filter — which is the setup's entire stated thesis — is dead, and only the entry model is left to test.

**Step 3 — The BOS entry with NO type filter. (one day.)**
Run the entry on every qualifying session, both directions, against Null B1 and B2. This separates "the entry works" from "the classification works", and it is the reusable asset: a validated 5m BOS entry could be fed into the incumbent retest model regardless of what happens to Dalton's taxonomy. Most of the possible value in this whole exercise lives here.

**Step 4 — The full setup, plus the control that decides it. (half a day, given steps 2–3.)**
V1 and V2 on OTD/ORR days only, against Null B2, **and** the identical run on rejected (OD/OA) days as Null C. The accepted-minus-rejected difference is the number that settles whether the open type is worth anything. Report it with its standard error; do not report the accepted rate on its own.

**Step 5 — Exit and parameter stability, only if step 4 survives. (half a day.)**
RR ∈ {1.0, 1.5, 2.0} (the authors' three), stop buffer ∈ {0, 2, 5}, fractal L=R ∈ {1, 2, 3}, stop variant V-S2. Read it as a **plateau check**, not a search: what matters is whether every cell is positive with smooth curves (as the incumbent's parameter table is), not whether one cell is best.

**Step 6 — Only then: out-of-sample split, per-year, long/short, and correlation of the daily R series with the incumbent NQ retest model.**
The incumbent runs +0.146R at PF 1.34 over 349 trades. A second leg is worth adding only if it is genuinely uncorrelated with it — that, and not its standalone expectancy, is the reason to keep it. Compute the correlation of per-session R before deciding.

**Kill criteria, written into the report header before the first run:** median cost/R > 0.08 at step 1; class lift < 3pp at step 2; z < 2.0 vs Null B2 at step 3 or 4; accepted-minus-rejected ≤ 0 at step 4. Any one of these ends the work and the negative result is written up in `reports/backtest_ib_bos.txt` — which, given that mechanising this material has already produced 51.6% against a claimed 72%, is the most likely outcome and is still worth having on the record.
