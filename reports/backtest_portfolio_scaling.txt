===== WHERE THE REMAINING PROFIT IS: LEGS, NOT TUNING =====

Per-trade expectancy is exhausted. Seven instruments, 21,672 filter
combinations, every exit scheme, and this week the whole win-rate and
stop-distance family - nothing moves +0.146R higher. What is NOT exhausted is
the NUMBER of uncorrelated legs, and that axis has a known scaling law.

--- STEP 1: RISK WEIGHTING BETWEEN THE TWO LEGS IS A DEAD END ---
  w on leg1     R/mo    DD_R    MAR   IS MAR  OOS MAR
         0%   +1.112    8.65   1.54     1.66     2.08
        25%   +1.123    4.34   3.11     3.25     4.51
        50%   +1.134    5.30   2.57     2.60     5.63
        65%   +1.140    6.21   2.20     2.32     5.87   <- min-variance weight
       100%   +1.155    8.32   1.67     1.58     6.78

Return per month is FLAT across every weight (+1.112 to +1.155), because both
legs happen to earn almost the same monthly R. Weighting therefore moves only
the drawdown - and the drawdown optimum disagrees with itself: in-sample it sits
at 25% on leg 1, out-of-sample it rises monotonically to 100%, and the
theoretical min-variance weight (65%) is worse than an even split on both.
Three answers, three directions. Maximum drawdown is a single-path statistic
with enormous sampling error, and fitting a weight to it is fitting noise. Keep
50/50, which needs no justification.

--- STEP 2: A THIRD LEG, BUILT FROM THE ONE UNUSED VALIDATED RULE ---
Leg 3 is the IB-width rule from the open-type work: on days whose first hour is
at least 0.9% of price wide, take the first 5-minute close beyond the Initial
Balance, stop at the IB mid, target 1R. Width as a PERCENTAGE, never in points -
an absolute threshold on an index is a date filter in disguise.

  leg                            n      WR     PF     expR     R/mo    DD_R    MAR
  leg 1 retest M1 1R           326   56.1%   1.37   +0.156   +1.155    8.32   1.67
  leg 2 IB confluence 1.5R     744   50.8%   1.19   +0.073   +1.228    9.21   1.60
  leg 3 IB extension wide      243   52.3%   1.13   +0.047   +0.272    9.03   0.36

Leg 3 is much weaker than the 83-trade version in the earlier note (+0.100R,
PF 1.53) - a different stop, and 243 trades instead of 83. The stronger reading
does not survive a cleaner definition, so the weak one stands.

  monthly correlation:  corr(1,2) = +0.029   corr(1,3) = -0.184   corr(2,3) = +0.129

Leg 3 is ANTICORRELATED with leg 1. That is the interesting property, not its
expectancy: a leg does not have to be good to help, it has to be non-negative
and uncorrelated. And the weight curve is dead flat -

  weight on leg 3:     0%     10%     20%     30%     40%
  MAR                2.82   2.84    2.86    2.88    2.91

- so leg 3 is free, and worth almost nothing. It buys a smoother ride (drawdown
5.07R -> 4.22R at a 20% weight) and no extra profit.

--- STEP 3: THE ONLY HONEST COMPARISON - MATCHED DRAWDOWN ---
Every row scaled so its maximum drawdown is exactly 10R, the budget in the
brief. This is what "more profitable" has to mean; R/mo at unmatched risk
compares nothing.

  legs             R/mo   MAR  scale  R/mo@10R   +mo     IS    OOS
  leg 1 only     +1.155  1.67   1.20    +1.388   61%   1.58   6.78
  leg 2 only     +1.228  1.60   1.09    +1.334   59%   1.59   2.65
  1+2 equal      +1.192  2.82   1.97    +2.350   68%   2.74   6.92
  1+2+3 equal    +0.881  2.89   2.73    +2.409   64%   2.85   9.71
  1+2+3 40/40/20 +1.005  2.86   2.37    +2.380   64%   2.80   8.51

Two legs nearly DOUBLE the money at the same drawdown: +1.39 -> +2.35 R/mo. The
third leg adds +0.06 R/mo, which is nothing. Positive months rise from 61% to
68% and the worst month improves from -5.07R to -2.64R.

--- STEP 4: WHAT THE TARGET ACTUALLY REQUIRES ---
For n uncorrelated legs of equal quality, MAR grows as sqrt(n). Measured against
that law: 2 legs read MAR 2.82 where sqrt(2) predicts 2.36 - better than the law,
because correlation is +0.03 rather than 0. Three legs read 2.89 where sqrt(3)
predicts 2.89 - exactly the law, and only because leg 3 is weak.

  legs of leg-1 quality    MAR     R/mo at a 10R drawdown
       1                  1.67          +1.39
       2                  2.36          +1.96   (measured 2.82 -> +2.35)
       3                  2.89          +2.40   (measured 2.89 -> +2.41)
       5                  3.73          +3.10   <- the 3R/mo end of the brief
      12                  5.77          +4.81
      17                  6.87          +5.72   <- the 6R/mo end of the brief

So: 3R/mo at a 10R drawdown needs about FIVE uncorrelated legs of this quality.
We have two and a half. 6R/mo needs about SEVENTEEN, which is a research
department, not a prop challenge. The 3-6R target was never a tuning problem; it
is a headcount problem, and the top of that range is out of reach.

--- THE WAY OUT, CONCRETELY ---
1. STOP TUNING THESE TWO. Every axis inside a single leg is measured and closed.
   Further search there produces false positives, not profit.
2. SIZE TO THE DRAWDOWN BUDGET, NOT TO HABIT. The two legs at 0.5% each is a
   5.1% historical drawdown against a 10% limit - half the budget unused. The
   free doubling is in position size, not in a better signal. Not to the limit
   though: 10R at 1% per R touches 10% exactly, and a historical maximum is a
   floor on the future one. 0.6-0.7% per leg is the defensible ceiling.
3. ADD LEG 3 AT A SMALL WEIGHT (20%) for the drawdown, and expect no profit
   from it. Its value is the -0.18 correlation with leg 1.
4. LEGS 4 AND 5 HAVE TO COME FROM A NEW COST STRUCTURE. Equity indices are
   done - the reason only NQ works is cost/R (NQ 1.4%, everything else 3-24%).
   The untested instrument class with comparably low cost/R is FX majors and
   gold. Running the two EXISTING models there is the next real experiment, and
   it is the only one left with a plausible path to +3R/mo.

--- WHAT NOT TO BELIEVE FROM THIS FILE ---
Six weightings and three leg combinations were tried; the differences among the
three-leg rows (MAR 2.84-2.89) are far inside the noise. The claim that
survives is the coarse one - two legs beat one by a factor of 1.7 at matched
drawdown, and it holds in-sample (2.74) and out-of-sample (6.92). The fine
weighting choices do not survive and are not claimed.

===== ADDENDUM: THE SAME MODEL ON MANY MARKETS DOES NOT DIVERSIFY =====

The scaling law says MAR grows with the number of uncorrelated legs. Three
models on NQ confirmed it. The obvious next question is whether the SAME model
on several markets works the same way - it would be free, since the rule is
already written.

It does not, and the reason is worth more than the result.

  the drive model, one rule, seven markets: monthly correlation
          NQ    ES   RTY    YM   DAX  SX5E  FTSE
  NQ    1.00  0.15  0.38  0.36  0.28 -0.00 -0.20
  ES    0.15  1.00  0.25  0.33  0.10 -0.10 -0.30
  RTY   0.38  0.25  1.00  0.28  0.26  0.21 -0.01
  YM    0.36  0.33  0.28  1.00  0.41  0.00 -0.17
  DAX   0.28  0.10  0.26  0.41  1.00  0.17 -0.03
  SX5E -0.00 -0.10  0.21  0.00  0.17  1.00  0.21
  FTSE -0.20 -0.30 -0.01 -0.17 -0.03  0.21  1.00

  mean pairwise correlation +0.12

That is LOWER than the +0.14 between the three models on NQ. By the
correlation argument alone these baskets should diversify at least as well.

  basket                        R/mo    DD_R    MAR   R/mo at 10R DD
  NQ alone                    +1.835   12.07   1.82        +1.520
  NQ+RTY                      +1.460   13.02   1.35        +1.121
  NQ+RTY+ES                   +1.032   16.04   0.77        +0.643
  all four US indices         +0.509   15.30   0.40        +0.333
  all seven                   -0.309   16.22   -0.23       -0.190
  NQ+DAX (different session)  +0.855   11.07   0.93        +0.772

  three models on NQ alone    +1.408    3.80   4.45        +3.706

Every basket is worse than NQ alone, and the whole seven-market version is
NEGATIVE. The best of 56 pairs and triples is NQ+RTY at MAR 1.35, still well
below the 1.82 of trading NQ by itself - and that is a cherry-picked upper
bound, not a finding.

THE LESSON: low correlation is necessary for diversification and nowhere near
sufficient. Every leg must ALSO be individually positive. Diluting a +0.089R
model with a -0.145R model does not buy a smoother curve; it buys a smaller
numerator against a drawdown that barely falls, because the drawdown of a
losing leg is not a random walk around zero - it trends down.

This also reframes the cost/R constraint that has governed this whole project.
The problem with ES, YM, DAX, EuroStoxx and FTSE is not that they are worse
versions of NQ that a portfolio could average out. It is that they cannot be
diversified INTO at all. Seven markets do not give seven legs; NQ gives one, and
the other six are dead weight however little they correlate with it.

So the route to more legs stays what it was: more models on NQ, or a market
whose cost/R genuinely resembles NQ's - which among index futures there is not.
