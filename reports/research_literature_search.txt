## 1. Which survivor plausibly beats 1.16R/month at <10R DD?

**None.** State the incumbent's real figure first, because it's the bar everything else fails:

13.9R/yr against 10.3R MaxDD = **MAR 1.35**; per-trade SD = 0.052·√349 = 0.97R, so monthly Sharpe = 1.16/(0.97·√7.9) = 0.42, **annualised Sharpe ≈ 1.47**. That is better than every honest out-of-sample number in the survivor set.

| Candidate | Best honest OOS | Return/MaxDD per yr |
|---|---|---|
| Incumbent NQ retest | +0.146R, SR 1.47 | **1.35** |
| Quantitativo NQ (90d/3%/8x) | 24.3% / MDD 24%, **no OOS at all** | 1.01 |
| Zarattini SPY headline (final+vol target) | 19.6% / MDD 25% | 0.78 |
| Brusco ES base (real 2024-26 OOS) | 7.8% / MDD 16.3% | 0.48 |
| Brusco ES final (real OOS) | −2.1% / MDD 22.7% | negative |
| Turn-of-Month ES net | ~1.5%/yr | ~0.1 |
| FOMC (decayed, 9.2bp×8) | ~0.7%/yr | ~0.1 |
| Payday / OpEx / Pre-holiday | 0.4–2.6%/yr gross, unreplicated or futures-failed | <0.3 |

The closest thing to a real candidate is the **Noise Area BASE variant on NQ** (not the headline variant), and it is worth stating mechanically because it is the answer to Q4:

- σ(t,HH:MM) = mean over the prior 90 sessions of |Close(d,HH:MM)/Open(d,09:30) − 1|
- UB = max(Open₀₉:₃₀, PrevClose₁₆:₀₀)·(1+σ); LB = min(…)·(1−σ)
- Decisions only at HH:00/HH:30, 10:00–15:30 ET. Flat: P>UB → long, P<LB → short.
- **Base exit: only on a cross of the opposite band, which also reverses.** No VWAP trail.
- Hard flat at 16:00. Size = floor(AUM·min(4, 0.02/σ_daily)/(Open·20)).

**Cost/R is not the problem here, and that matters.** With the opposite-band stop, R = UB−LB ≈ 2σ·price ≈ 260 NQ points at midday. Round trip at futures tick = 0.44 pts → **cost/R = 0.17%**; on a prop CFD at 1.34 spread + 1.0 slippage = 2.34 pts → **0.9%**. Both sit *below* NQ's already-survivable 1.4%, i.e. an order of magnitude under the ~4% cliff your own table locates between DAX (+0.009R) and ES (−0.087R). Frequency is 0.61 trades/day (Brusco: 326 trades / 530 ES days) ≈ 13/month, 1.6× the incumbent.

But its measured MAR is 0.48 — 2.8× worse than what you already hold. **It clears the cost test and fails the return/drawdown test.** So: no survivor qualifies.

## 2. Search modality not run

**The holding-horizon dimension.** Every model you tested — ORB, retest, sweep, and every literature candidate you evaluated — is intraday-flat-by-close. You searched signals × instruments × parameters × sessions, but never horizon.

This matters because cost/R = cost / stop-distance, cost per round trip is fixed by the venue, and you already established cost/R is *the* binding constraint. Stop distance grows roughly with √(holding time); cost does not grow at all. Concretely, on the same seven markets:

| Market | cost/R at 5-min OR | cost/R at 2×ATR(14 daily) |
|---|---|---|
| NQ | 1.4% | ~0.05% |
| DAX | 4.5% | ~0.4% |
| ES | 6.1% | ~0.5% |
| FTSE | 10.0% | ~1.0% |
| EuroStoxx | 23.8% | ~2.4% |

**A multi-day horizon makes all seven of your dead markets cost-survivable**, including EuroStoxx, which is currently 17× over the cliff. That is a 10–30× improvement on the exact variable you identified as binding, and it is the only lever available: you already used futures-tick costs, so the cost numerator cannot be reduced further.

What it would likely find: not a new signal, but the *same* structural signals working where they currently can't. The honest prediction is time-series momentum / multi-day breakout / carry at Sharpe 0.7–1.1 per market with correlation 0.5–0.8 across equity indices, ~0.2 once you add FX, rates and commodities.

A note on the instrument-swap alternative, which looks attractive and isn't: NQ's 1.4% is exceptional because of a very high index level against a 0.25-point tick and a deep book. Run the arithmetic on the obvious substitutes — EURUSD raw (~0.7 pip round-trip commission-equivalent against a 10–15 pip 5-min OR ≈ 5%), XAUUSD (~$0.25 against a $4 OR ≈ 6%) — and they land in ES/YM territory, not NQ territory. The only intraday instruments in NQ's cost/R class are NQ, MNQ, and possibly BTC/ETH perpetuals on a low-fee venue (0.5bp against a ~0.4% OR ≈ 1.2%). **Instrument substitution buys you nothing intraday; horizon extension buys you 10–30×.**

## 3. A class with a fundamentally different profile

Yes, and it is not higher frequency — it is **breadth at a longer horizon**.

Higher frequency is unavailable: the only way to trade more often without spread scaling is to *earn* the spread rather than pay it, which requires queue priority you cannot get on a prop CFD account with a deviation cap.

The class that fits the cost structure is **multi-day diversified time-series momentum / breakout across 15–25 futures or CFD markets** (equity indices, FX majors, rates, energy, metals), 5–40 day holds, stop at 2–3×ATR(14 daily), 0.25% risk per market, monthly or signal-driven rebalance. Cost/R 0.05–0.5%, i.e. costs become a rounding error rather than the deciding term. Century-scale out-of-sample evidence (Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen; Hurst–Ooi–Pedersen), which is a different evidential class from anything in your survivor file.

**The honest catch, and it is the crux of Q5:** portfolio Sharpe scales as √N in *independent* bets, so 20 markets at Sharpe 0.8 and correlation 0.2 gives portfolio Sharpe ~1.6 — comparable to what you already have. What it buys is not a bigger edge but *more bets per unit calendar time*, which is exactly what a fixed-deadline ruin race needs. What it costs is drawdown duration: TSMOM drawdowns run 12–24 months, which is compatible with a 10% static cap only if there is no time limit, and incompatible with any fast-pass requirement.

Also worth flagging: **your portfolio test does not refute diversification.** You added legs with measured edges of +0.013R and −0.025R — i.e. nothing — so DD rose 10.3R→35.0R and return/DD fell 0.113→0.041 exactly as arithmetic predicts. That result refutes diversifying *into zero*, not diversification. The correct test is N legs each independently clearing +0.10R, which you have never had more than one of.

## 5. The case that no such edge exists, and what follows

**The argument, in four steps:**

1. **Pass probability is a function of Sharpe alone; leverage cancels.** A prop challenge sets the target (+10%) and the ruin barrier (−10%) in the same units. Scaling risk scales both. So P(pass) depends only on the strategy's Sharpe over the challenge horizon and the number of independent bets inside it — which is precisely why your Monte Carlo saturates at 57% with 43% blow-ups *regardless of leverage*. That is not an implementation failure; it is the correct answer to the question you asked. To reach 80%+ in a one-month window you need annualised Sharpe ≈ 3.

2. **No replicated intraday index strategy has Sharpe 3 net of costs.** The best honest OOS numbers in your entire survivor file are 1.24–1.34 (Zarattini, static size) and 1.11 (Brusco replication) — and both went to ~0 or negative in 2025–26 on two instruments and two independent data vendors at 0.97 daily correlation. Your own measured 1.47 is *the best number in the file*.

3. **Four independent multiple-testing-corrected sweeps return nulls on exactly this market.** Sullivan–Timmermann–White: 7,846 rules, nothing on DJIA/S&P/S&P futures. Hsu–Kuan: 39,832 rules including adaptive meta-strategies, nothing on mature large caps (only NASDAQ Composite and Russell 2000 survive, attributed to market immaturity). Carchano–Pardo: 188 cyclical effects on ES/DAX/Nikkei futures, 1 survivor at ~1.5%/yr net. Mesfin: 14 intraday families on MNQ 2021–25, none above friction. Your own ORB holdout, where the best-fitted filters inverted to worst, is a fifth instance of the same result.

4. **Every candidate with a genuine post-publication OOS test decayed.** Gao et al. → Rosa (JFM 2022, "predictability disappears"), Baltussen (ES R²_OOS −1.68), QuantConnect (SR −0.628). Bondarenko–Muravyev → NY Fed, "The Disappearing Overnight Drift," 2026. Zarattini → Brusco, negative on both instruments since 2025. This is the McLean–Pontiff base rate operating on the most competed intraday market in the world.

**What follows: neither of your two options as stated.**

Do not scale ambition down to 0.58%/month, and do not change instrument class. **Change the constraint, not the strategy.** MAR 1.35 at Sharpe 1.47 is a top-decile result — most professional CTAs run MAR 0.3–0.7. It fails only against a one-month 1:1 ruin race, which is a format problem, not an edge problem. Specifically:

- **Deploy the NQ retest against a mandate it can win**: own capital, or a firm with a static (not trailing) drawdown and no time limit. At Sharpe 1.47 a 10% target with a 10% barrier and unlimited time is a waiting problem with high pass probability, not a coin flip. Do not attempt the one-month format — your own Monte Carlo says 0%, and that number is structural.
- **Spend the next unit of effort on horizon, not on more intraday signals.** It is the only lever that moves cost/R by 10–30×, it resurrects all five markets you killed on cost, and it is the one dimension of the search space you have never touched. Concretely: re-run your existing model family on daily bars with 2–3×ATR stops across the seven indices you already have data for, then extend the universe to FX/rates/commodities for uncorrelated breadth.
- **Run the Noise Area base variant on NQ once** (Q4), because it is cheap and it is the only intraday candidate not yet falsified in the form that currently works.
- **Only add portfolio legs that independently clear +0.10R.** Your 10.3R→35.0R result is the correct penalty for doing otherwise, and it will recur.

The realistic ceiling under any 10% drawdown cap is roughly 8–13%/yr regardless of strategy, because MAR tops out near 1.4 across everything documented. Any plan that requires 10% in a month is requiring a MAR the literature does not contain.
