## B. THE MECHANICAL GAP — 55.6% vs 76.4%

Ranked by how much each can carry. The first two probably account for all of it.

**1. The 85-trade sample was hand-curated and the stop was placed with the completed chart visible. Say it plainly — his own slide admits it.** Slide 03/06 (frame 47) attaches *"гибкая постановка SL (в зависимости от контекста)"* to the approach in writing. He describes moving the stop from the far IB boundary in to the IB 0.5 on exactly the wide-IB days [12:27]–[12:43]. Provenance: [75:17] — the whole thing was built manually in about six evenings of 1.5–2 hours, i.e. **~2 minutes per recorded trade** across ~500 instrument-sessions, judged trade by trade in bar replay. The smoking gun is internal: his **NQ column is 70.8 / 37.5 / 22.2%** at 1:1 / 1:1.5 / 1:2, against driftless breakevens of 50 / 40 / 33.3%. A real directional edge cannot beat the random-walk floor by 20.8 points at 1R and then fall *below* it at both higher multiples. That signature is precisely what discretionary stop-distance selection produces: it inflates the 1R hit rate and destroys everything beyond it. Max losing streak of 2 in 72 trades at a ~29% loss rate is the same story.

**2. Scope. We compared to the wrong number.** 76.4% is **fDAX only, calendar 2024 only, Xetra hours only, 85 hand-recorded Open-Type trades**. The NQ figure is **70.8% on 72 trades** (slide 04/06). There is a *third*, larger, entirely untested claim beside it: **110 fDAX IB-extension trades at 72% / 50.4% / 31.5%**, with the flexible-SL note attached. On n=72, one standard error is ~5.4 points, so the honest comparison is 55.6% vs 70.8% ± 11, on one year vs three and a half, one market vs seven. Still a gap, but not the gap we cited. His own slide also fails arithmetic: 110 + 85 = 195, slide says 196; the extension percentages do not resolve to integer wins on n=110.

**3. Stop location, and therefore R itself.** Default stop is the **opposite Initial Balance boundary** [12:23], so R ≈ one IB width. On-screen position tools: 0.55% of price (frames 102/103, NQ, entry 14144.50 / stop 14066.75 / target 14222.25, exact 1:1), 0.90% and 1.28% (frames 116, 141), 0.545% (frame 245, FDAX). Our models size risk from a scaled window extreme. A materially larger R raises any 1:1 hit rate mechanically **and** improves cost/R — the binding constraint on this project. This is the one difference that changes the object being measured rather than sub-selecting sessions.

**4. Entry trigger and its timing.** Not a fixed bar: classify on the first 5-minute candle as a *direction filter only* [62:24]–[63:01], then require a sweep of a prior high/low, a fractal, a pullback, and a **5-minute BOS body close** [06:05], [13:10]–[13:22], with left-side levels needing the *second* push. He explicitly excludes inversion-style entries. Our mechanical classifier fires on every qualifying session at a fixed offset.

**5. Session anchor and IB length.** fDAX from the Xetra cash open, NQ from RTH (slide 02/06, frame 25). **The IB length is never stated aloud in 76 minutes** — eleven mentions of "инишенал баланс", not one duration; read off the shaded indicator box as 60 minutes / twelve 5m bars by three independent agents.

**6. Nothing demonstrated is in the counted sample.** The NQ walkthrough runs on Feb–Mar 2022, the fDAX walkthrough on May 2023; both backtests are 2024. At [51:14] he says outright of an on-screen example: *"такого бактеста не будет — я выделял конкретный setup"*.

## E. BOTTOM LINE

**No. There is nothing here a mechanical tester should expect to pay off, and specifically nothing that rejects trades and cuts drawdown.**

The reason is structural, not a failure of the first reading. The second pass was thorough — full transcript read end to end by multiple passes, native-resolution frames pulled and upscaled where the 1280px stills were illegible, the entire 7-page slide deck transcribed, every rejection verb grepped in Russian. There is no drawdown-reducing filter in this recording because there is no *quantified* rejection in this recording. The one relative threshold he states — two hours from the open — was already in production, derived, DST-safe, at `tools/markets.py:312`, before anyone watched the video.
