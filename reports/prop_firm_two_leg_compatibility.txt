===== CAN THE TWO-LEG SYSTEM BE TRADED AT FUNDING PIPS AND FTMO? =====

Two questions, and they have different answers: is it technically possible, and
is it permitted. A third question settles both.

--- 1. TECHNICALLY: YES ON BOTH, checked directly in the terminals ---
mt5.account_info().margin_mode on the live accounts:

  Funding Pips  login 20300017  FundingPips-SIM1   margin_mode = 2 = RETAIL_HEDGING
  FTMO          login 1514461324  FTMO-Demo        margin_mode = 2 = RETAIL_HEDGING

Both can hold a long and a short on the same instrument at once.

A TRAP FOR OLDER FTMO ACCOUNTS. FTMO only began creating challenges as HEDGING
accounts for purchases after 30 January 2026, 01:00. Anything bought before that
is a NETTING account, where a long and a short on the same symbol cancel each
other out - the second order reduces or closes the first instead of opening a
new position. On a netting account the two legs cannot both exist in opposite
directions at all, regardless of the rulebook. Check margin_mode on the real
account, not on a demo.

On hedging accounts margin is charged INDEPENDENTLY for the long and the short,
so opposite positions consume roughly double the margin rather than netting to
nothing.

Other account limits, neither of them binding at our sizes:
  Funding Pips  limit_orders 30    GER40 volume_limit 100 lots, volume_max 10
  FTMO          limit_orders 200   GER40.cash volume_limit none, volume_max 1000

--- 2. BY THE RULES: THEY DIFFER ---
FTMO: same-account hedging is permitted, cross-account hedging is not. Holding
long and short on the same instrument simultaneously is explicitly allowed on
accounts created after 30 Jan 2026. Total allocation is capped at $400,000 per
trader or strategy, and running the same strategy across several accounts can
trigger suspension.

Funding Pips: their published Trading Conduct page returns HTTP 403 to automated
fetch, so the primary text could not be read here - this needs your own reading
or a written answer from support. Every third-party summary found states that
Funding Pips prohibits hedging, long-short arbitrage, reverse arbitrage and
opposite-account trading outright, and at least one reads the prohibition as
covering same-account long-and-short as well. The cross-account ban is
unambiguous everywhere; the same-account case is where the sources hedge their
language, which is exactly the point that would matter.

--- 3. THE QUESTION THAT SETTLES IT: THE LEGS NEVER OPPOSE ---
Measured across 905 sessions, every leg-1 trade classified against what leg 2
was doing at that moment:

  leg-1 trades                                349
  day had no leg-2 setup at all                74   (21%)
  no overlap in time                          248   (71%)
  overlapped, SAME direction                   27    (8%)
  overlapped, OPPOSITE direction                0    (0%)

Zero. Not once in 905 sessions does the system hold a long and a short at the
same time.

The reason is structural, not luck. Leg 1 enters between 09:35 and 11:30 with a
median hold of six minutes, and three quarters of its entries land in the first
half hour - so it is usually flat before 10:30, when leg 2 opens. And when they
do overlap, they agree: leg 1 needs price above its frozen SMA89 and beyond the
opening range, which is the same condition that puts the IB close above its
midpoint.

Honest bound on that zero: it was zero out of 27 opportunities to disagree, so
the rule of three puts the true disagreement rate under about 11% of overlaps -
which is under 0.3% of all days. Rare, not impossible.

--- CONCLUSION ---
The two-leg system needs no hedging, so it is tradeable at both firms, and the
Funding Pips hedging prohibition does not bite it.

To remove the residual 0.3% entirely, add one rule at no measured cost:

    If leg 2 is open and leg 1 signals the OPPOSITE direction, skip leg 1.

Applied to the whole history this rule skips zero trades, so it costs nothing
and makes the system provably hedge-free rather than merely observed to be.

Still worth confirming in writing with Funding Pips, because it is their call
and not mine: whether two SAME-direction positions on one instrument, opened by
two different systems minutes apart, is read as one strategy or as two. That
happens on 8% of leg-1 trades and is not hedging, but their trade-pattern
analysis flags "identical entry times, sizes and pairs" and it is cheaper to ask
than to find out after a payout request.
