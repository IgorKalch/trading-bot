===== TYPE OPEN + INITIAL BALANCE (Garry / Hnurewnik) =====

Source: TO_IB/iborrotd.mp4, a 76-minute screen recording showing the PDF
"Garry_Hnurewnik_Type Open + Initial Balance Setup". Frames extracted with
ffmpeg and read directly. Same Hnurewnik as the original ORB material.

--- WHAT THE SOURCE CLAIMS ---
Slide 2: "we take only two open types, Open Test Drive and Open Rejection
Reverse, and the interaction of those types with the previous day's profile
within the formation of the Initial Balance".

Slide 3: "the main idea is NOT to use types we cannot identify as OTD/ORR,
because then we have a high chance of not getting price extension". Two
backtests, FDAX and NQ, one year, Initial Balance and a 5-minute chart, entry
model 5m BOS, FDAX from Xetra and NQ from RTH.

Slide 4, FDAX 2024, Xetra hours, 196 positions:
    110 IB extension     RR 1:1 = 72%    1:1.5 = 50.4%   1:2 = 31.5%
     85 Open Type        RR 1:1 = 76.4%  1:1.5 = 52.9%   1:2 = 45.88%
The first approach is described as classic IB extension "with FLEXIBLE stop
placement depending on context".

At 1:1 a 76.4% win rate is +0.528R per trade before costs. That would be three
and a half times the best edge this project has measured anywhere.

--- FINDING 1: THE PREMISE IS ALMOST ALWAYS TRUE ---
Measured over 907 NQ and 940 DAX sessions, first hour as the Initial Balance:

                          NQ      DAX
  IB extends either way  96.7%   97.2%
  extends up             62.4%   65.9%
  extends down           55.5%   59.6%
  BOTH sides (whipsaw)   21.2%   28.2%
  one side only          75.5%   69.0%

"The IB will extend" is not information. It happens 97% of the time. The only
question is direction, and the only real risk is the 21-28% of days that take
both sides.

The reversal-type open proxy - an excursion of at least 15% of the IB width in
the first 30 minutes, followed by trading back through the open - barely moves
it: 97.3% against 95.7% on NQ. A 1.6 point difference on the variable the whole
setup is built around.

--- FINDING 2: WHAT ACTUALLY SEPARATES DAYS IS IB WIDTH ---
                        extends   BOTH sides
  NQ  narrow IB (<med)   98.7%      28.9%
  NQ  wide IB   (>=med)  94.7%      13.4%
  DAX narrow IB          98.5%      34.5%
  DAX wide IB            96.0%      21.9%

A wide first hour halves the chance of a two-sided day. That is the opposite of
the folk claim that a narrow IB predicts a trend day, and it is the one
conditioning variable in this material that survives measurement.

Against the source's claimed 72% at 1:1 on FDAX, the mechanical version measures
51.6%. A twenty point gap.

The likely explanation is in the source's own slide: "flexible stop placement
depending on context". A discretionary stop is precisely how a 72% win rate at
1:1 is produced - the stop goes where it will not be hit, which shrinks R after
the fact and cannot be reproduced by any rule.

--- A TRAP CAUGHT WHILE TESTING ---
The first version of the width filter used absolute points, and NQ at min 120
points read PF 1.50 with a 3.1R drawdown and its interval above zero. It was
largely a DATE filter: the share of days with IB wider than 120 points runs
34.8% in 2023, 52.9% in 2024, 70.4% in 2025 and 92.9% in 2026, because the index
went from 12,000 to 29,000. Median IB width as a PERCENTAGE of price is stable
across those years at 0.64-0.81%, so the filter was rewritten as a percentage
(filters.min_or_width_pct) and the artefact disappears.

The percentage version is the honest one: NQ at 0.9%, 83 trades, PF 1.53,
+0.100R, drawdown 2.0R, and all four years positive - but the interval still
includes zero at 83 trades.
